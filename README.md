# Emmy Noether — mathematical works in translation

Editions of Emmy Noether's collected mathematical works: the German source text, eleven complete AI-translated editions in nine languages (Chinese and Interslavic each in two scripts), and partial translations in five more languages. Each complete edition contains papers 1-43; the lectures "Algebra der hyperkomplexen Größen" (winter semester 1929/30) written up by M. Deuring; H. Kapferer's paper with its supplement written jointly with E. Noether; bibliography and the lists of short communications, book reviews and books produced with Emmy Noether's participation.

Every complete edition is published as two text layers, each as a PDF, a single editable LaTeX file and a source archive:

- **Base text** keeps the readings of the original printings; editorial notes discuss the readings in question.
- **Text with emendations** applies the editorial corrections; each correction is explained in an editorial note.

## German source text

The translations follow the German working text of the project, edition NOETH-DE-ED-0028 (Zenodo version "2026-09-05 r40"): [PDF, 466 pp.](https://zenodo.org/records/22342039/files/10-de.pdf) · [LaTeX](https://zenodo.org/records/22342039/files/20-de.tex) · [Zenodo record](https://zenodo.org/records/22342039) (doi:10.5281/zenodo.22342039). AI-transcribed LaTeX text of the original printings, with editorial corrections recorded in German editions ED0001-ED0028; the Zenodo record lists "AI typesetting & translation" and "TTP" as contributors and describes the text as a working source restoration, not an official or peer-reviewed critical edition. Earlier German working sources are kept in [modern-latex-manuscripts/sources/noether](https://github.com/KokunoYumeto/modern-latex-manuscripts/tree/main/sources/noether).

## Authors and provenance

The mathematical works are by Emmy Noether and the co-authors named in them: Werner Schmeidler (Paper 17), R. Brauer (Paper 32), and R. Brauer and H. Hasse (Paper 38). The 1929/30 lectures were written up by M. Deuring. Work 45 is by H. Kapferer, with a supplement written jointly with E. Noether. Original publication details, signatures and citations are given in the texts.

## Known differences between editions

Some papers were translated from an earlier English translation whose formulas differ from the German text in places:

| Edition | Papers |
|---|---|
| French | parts of 24, 30, 31 and 34 |
| Chinese, Simplified and Traditional characters | 24, 31, 32 and 34; parts of 35 and 40 |
| Spanish | 23, 24, 30, 31, 32, 34 and 37; parts of 35 and 40 (in Paper 31, section 8.2 is shortened) |
| Russian, Ukrainian, Interslavic (both scripts) | 23, 24, 30, 31, 32, 34, 37 and 41–43; parts of 35 and 40 (Paper 43 is about a third of the full paper; Papers 41 and 42 are about four fifths) |
| Japanese | 32, 34 and 40; parts of 30, 35, 41 and 42 (Papers 40 and 41 are about three quarters) |

Further differences:

- Paper 2: in two places the English, Spanish, Japanese, Russian, Ukrainian and Interslavic editions read ĵ where the German text reads ϑ̂.
- Papers 1 and 2: the English edition and the editions translated from it may lack some multiplication dots of the German text.
- Paper 2: in the Spanish, Russian, Ukrainian and Interslavic editions, three footnotes of the German text (the note on Σ₃ and the citations of Gordan–Kerschensteiner, pp. 92 and 134) are marked only by an asterisk or attached to a neighbouring sentence; in the Japanese edition this applies to the note on Σ₃.
- Paper 15, display (7): every translation writes the substitution ξ14 = ξ13 only; the German display also substitutes ξ24 = ξ23, while the German text before it names only ξ14 = ξ13.
- Paper 30: the notation may differ between editions in places.
- Japanese: some readings in Papers 10, 13 and 38 may differ from the German text.
- Spanish: some readings in Paper 20 and in the 1929/30 lectures may differ from the German text.
- Chinese (both scripts): in Paper 38 a Fraktur 𝔭 may be printed as p.

The PDFs of the following editions have no bookmarks: Spanish; Russian; Ukrainian; Interslavic, Latin script; Interslavic, Cyrillic script; Japanese; and the German source text.
